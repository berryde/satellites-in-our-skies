## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment.** The scene is specific; the interface around it is not. The teal low-orbit shell, the thin pink geostationary ring and the white orbit line on selection could only be this product. The chrome is a default dark kit: translucent rounded panel, grey buttons, native select, native range input, system sans. It could sit on a video player unchanged. The largest miss is the timeline: seventy years of spaceflight is a bare slider with no years, no decades, no growth curve and no landmarks. The product's distinctive claim is "the complete history, playable", and the history control carries none of it. DESIGN.md's "small and unbranded on purpose" is defensible for buttons but has been applied to the one control that should carry the idea.

**Deterministic scan.** Source scan of `src` and `index.html`: 0 findings, but only the regex rules ran on 14 files; element-level rules need a rendered page. The purple/violet palette check is off because DESIGN.md declares `#a78bfa`. This agrees with the design review, which flagged the same footer independently. No false positives. The detector cannot see anything drawn in WebGL, and it reported nothing on touch-target sizes, which the review measured as undersized.

## Overall Impression

The first second on desktop is a real "oh", and then nothing moves. The best thing here, the count climbing from 1 to 20,097 as seven decades play, is behind a dropdown nobody is prompted to open. The single biggest opportunity is making the timeline the signature element and opening on motion.

## What's Working

1. **The count is the headline.** It is the largest text on screen, in tabular figures, and it climbs as time plays. It reads without any domain knowledge.
2. **Colour is spent only on data.** Four saturated hues on near-black with a dim Earth let the low-orbit shell and geostationary ring read as structure at once.
3. **Selection is well judged.** White ring, thin white orbit line, and the deep link turning the camera to face the satellite. The semantic skeleton underneath is sound too: h1/h2, `aria-pressed` toggles, labelled slider and select, a `dl` for details, Esc to close, visible default focus rings.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] "● Live" contradicts the product's honesty rule**
- **What:** A red dot with "Live", a "Go live" button and a "Real time" speed, on by default; the meta description says "on a live 3D globe". The only disclaimer, "Positions are illustrative", is 10px grey at 4.3:1, mid-sentence in the bottom-right corner. The ISS deep link draws the station over East Africa under a red Live badge.
- **Why it matters:** PRODUCT.md says nothing may imply real-time tracking. A visitor will believe that is where the ISS is.
- **Fix:** Rename to "Today" / "Jump to today" and drop the red dot until CelesTrak lands. Put a readable note where the claim is made (details card, beside the date): "Orbits are real; positions along them are illustrative." Fix the meta description.
- **Where:** `src/ui/TimeControls.tsx:15,44-46`, `src/state/store.ts:4`, `src/App.tsx:59-61`, `index.html:7`
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

**[P1] The showpiece is hidden behind the default speed and an anonymous slider**
- **What:** The page opens at real time, where motion is imperceptible. The time-lapse needs two unprompted actions. The slider has no years or landmarks; one pixel is about 41 days on desktop.
- **Why it matters:** The product succeeds when a visitor comes away impressed by motion and history. Most will see a still picture.
- **Fix:** Open at a visibly moving speed, or with a short skippable sweep from 1957 that respects reduced motion. Rebuild the timeline with decade ticks, "1957" and "Today" end labels, a satellites-in-orbit curve behind the track, and a few named moments. Cut the six speeds to three or four.
- **Where:** `src/state/store.ts:3-10,30`, `src/ui/TimeControls.tsx:49-58`
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable shape`, then `/impeccable bolder`

**[P1] Phone layout collides**
- **What:** At 390x844 the details card covers the title, count and the whole legend; the legend sits unbacked on dense dots; the footer overlaps the slider; the globe is cropped wider than the screen so the geostationary ring is never seen. In landscape the timeline covers a legend row and the card covers the speed and Live controls.
- **Why it matters:** Phone is a stated primary context, and selecting a satellite, the core interaction, buries the rest of the interface there.
- **Fix:** Below 640px make details a bottom sheet above the timeline; collapse the legend to a compact row; move credit and disclaimer into an "About" affordance; set camera altitude from the viewport aspect so the whole Earth fits.
- **Where:** `src/ui/SatelliteDetails.tsx:64`, `src/ui/Legend.tsx:18`, `src/ui/TimeControls.tsx:18`, `src/App.tsx:59`, `src/globe/SatelliteGlobe.tsx:35`
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

**[P1] Jargon and raw catalogue strings on a no-expertise product**
- **What:** "LEO / MEO / GEO", "payload", "Inclination", "Period", "NORAD ID" are unexplained. Sputnik 1 is "1-y ISZ". The count reads "1 satellites in orbit".
- **Why it matters:** Principle 2 is "No expertise required", and the legend, the only key to the picture, is three acronyms.
- **Fix:** "Low orbit", "Medium orbit", "Geostationary", "Elliptical", each with a one-line gloss. Phrase details as a person would ("Circles the Earth every 93 minutes"). Add common-name aliases in the pipeline. Use "satellites" everywhere and fix the singular.
- **Where:** `src/ui/Legend.tsx:20,31`, `src/ui/SatelliteDetails.tsx:49-61`, `src/App.tsx:45`, `pipeline/names.csv`
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

**[P2] Core content is pointer-only, with undersized targets and thin states**
- **What:** The canvas has no role, label or tab stop, so no satellite is reachable by keyboard or screen reader, and there is no search. Close is about 27x18px, legend rows 24px tall, timeline controls 28px, slider 16px. The slider has no `aria-valuetext` and announces epoch milliseconds. `prefers-reduced-motion` is ignored. The error state is developer-facing with no retry.
- **Why it matters:** Excludes keyboard and screen-reader users from the main content and makes phone use fiddly.
- **Fix:** Add "Find a satellite" search (also answers "where is the ISS?"). 44px touch targets. `aria-valuetext` with the formatted date. Start paused under reduced motion. In the card: "Not in orbit on this date" with a "Jump to launch" button. Friendly error with Retry.
- **Where:** `src/globe/SatelliteGlobe.tsx:162`, `src/ui/SatelliteDetails.tsx:73-80`, `src/ui/Legend.tsx:28`, `src/ui/TimeControls.tsx:49-58`, `src/state/store.ts:29`, `src/App.tsx:29`
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (first-timer):** Lands on a still image with no cue that the globe drags. Reads "LEO 18,053" with nowhere to learn what LEO is, and does not realise the rows are buttons. Sees "● Live" and concludes the positions are real. Presses Pause and nothing visibly changes. Drags to 1957: "1 satellites in orbit", an apparently empty globe, and the first satellite is called "1-y ISZ".

**Casey (phone):** The details card buries title, count and legend, with a 27x18px close target in the top-right, the worst thumb position. Every timeline control is 28px tall or less. The footer overlaps the slider. Nothing is written to the URL, so a tab reload resets time and selection. About 2.7 MB loads behind a bare "Loading satellites…".

**Sam (keyboard / screen reader):** Tab order is the four legend rows, Pause, speed, date, Close; the globe and every satellite are absent from it. The slider announces a raw number. "● Live" reads as "black circle Live". Nothing is announced when the card opens or the speed resets, and focus does not move to the card. Reduced motion is ignored.

## Minor Observations

- Live red and geostationary pink are close in hue; at 2.5px the pink ring reads as red.
- DESIGN.md's Two Weights Rule (400 and 600) conflicts with its own Go live spec at 500; the code follows 500.
- The hover label is not clamped to the viewport and will clip at the right and bottom edges.
- Selecting a satellite does not update the URL, so deep links cannot be produced from the interface.
- Minute precision in the date is noise at 1 year/s.
- Space does not toggle play.
- The legend block intercepts drags in the top-left; the header correctly passes them through.
- The details card pops in and out with no transition.

## Questions to Consider

1. If the distinctive claim is history, why open on "now, at real time", the one view where nothing happens, and not on 1957 with one dot and a Play button?
2. Until real positions ship, is "Live" worth having? What is lost if the present is just the right-hand end of the timeline, labelled "Today"?
3. Is "small and unbranded on purpose" a decision or the absence of one? What would the timeline look like if it were designed for this dataset?
